# Community governance

Security BSides Kraków is a community initiative. Organizers steward the organization and community resources; repository maintainers guide individual projects. Decisions should be understandable, welcoming and proportional to a volunteer community's capacity.

## Participation and decisions

Anyone can contribute to public repositories through Issues, Discussions and pull requests. Membership is not a prerequisite. Significant project changes should be discussed openly when they do not involve sensitive information. Record the decision, rationale and next step in the relevant Issue or Discussion.

Hacker Club proposals enter through Project Ideas. Native upvotes inform prioritization; organizers decide approval based on feasibility, safety, resources and community benefit. Approved projects have a linked tracking Issue and public Project status. Votes do not guarantee approval.

Maintainers may accept, request changes to, postpone or decline contributions, explaining their reasoning respectfully. Escalate unresolved project disagreements to organizers via contact@bsideskrakow.pl. Use the private conduct or security reporting process for sensitive concerns. People with a conflict of interest should ask another organizer to review the matter.

## Access model

| Role | Purpose | Intended access |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Organization owners | Account recovery, security policy, integrations and organization administration | Owner; keep the group small |
| Organizers team | Day-to-day stewardship of community repositories | Maintain on explicitly assigned public repositories |
| Project maintainers team | Code and documentation stewardship | Write on the project repository |
| Community triage team | Help newcomers and organize Issues | Triage on the project repository |
| Public contributors | Proposals, discussion and pull requests | Public participation; no membership required |

Teams are capability groups, not honorary titles. Owners assign people only after agreement on responsibilities. Empty teams confer no access to new people. Team membership does not automatically grant access to every repository. Organization members have no implicit repository permission; private repository access is explicit.

Owners manage repository creation/deletion, visibility changes, outside collaborator invitations and organization-level integrations. Request a repository by discussing its purpose, maintainer, intended visibility, license and expected data with organizers. Do not store private participant or sponsor data in public repositories.

Outside collaborator invitations are an owner-managed process. GitHub Free does not offer an organization-wide technical control restricting invitations to owners; repository admins can invite collaborators. Avoid granting repository Admin to non-owners when Maintain or Write is sufficient, and review invitations and access regularly.

## Continuity and accountability

Aim for at least two trusted owners with 2FA and securely stored recovery methods. The current owner must select and invite a second person; do not grant ownership merely to fill a quota. Review owners, teams, outside collaborators, apps and tokens at least quarterly and when someone leaves a role.

Remove access when responsibilities end, while crediting past contributions. Transfer maintenance or archive inactive projects with a clear explanation; do not silently delete community history. Owners should document exceptional administrative actions and avoid bypassing review for routine work.
