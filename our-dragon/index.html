Kraków, Poland / An origin story

THE DRAGON
BEHIND THE LOGO

Born in Kraków.
Raised in legend.
Reborn in cybersecurity.

If you have ever wondered why there is a dragon in the BSides Kraków logo, the answer is underneath Wawel Hill.

Meet our dragon ↓

Kraków / Wawel / Smok Wawelski / BSides

01 / Beneath the hill

Meet
Smok Wawelski

Every city has its stories.
Kraków has a dragon.

Smok Wawelski means “the Wawel Dragon”: one of Kraków’s best-known legendary inhabitants. Its home in local folklore is Smocza Jama, the Dragon’s Den, a real cave beneath Wawel Hill.

The story was already being written down in medieval Poland. One of its earliest recorded versions appears in Wincenty Kadłubek’s chronicle around the beginning of the 13th century. Later writers changed the characters and the ending.

A real place. A recorded tradition. A legendary creature. The chronicles preserve the story, not evidence that a dragon existed.

02 / The story passed down

The Legend

Here is the familiar cobbler retelling. It is one version of the tale, not a single, fixed medieval account.

  1. The dragon

    A city in its shadow.

    A monster lives below Wawel Hill. Kraków fears it; animals become its food. Confronting the creature is no easy answer.

  2. The sheep

    A different kind of weapon.

    A resourceful cobbler, known as Skuba in later retellings, offers a trap: a sheepskin packed with sulphur. The dragon takes the bait.

  3. The fire

    The trap takes hold.

    After swallowing the bait, the dragon is overcome by a burning thirst. It rushes to the nearby river for relief.

  4. The Vistula

    One drink too many.

    It drinks and drinks from the Vistula until it bursts. In this telling, ingenuity succeeds where strength cannot.

Skuba belongs to later tradition: Wawel Royal Castle dates his appearance to Joachim Bielski’s late-16th-century retelling. Earlier chronicles tell the dragon’s defeat differently.

03 / Myth meets the city

A Legend Older
Than the Conference

From medieval chronicles to Kraków folklore, the story has kept changing. Its geography has stayed close: Wawel Hill, the cave and the river.

Today, the dragon is one of the city’s most recognizable symbols. A story you can encounter on a walk through Kraków.

  1. Early 13th century

    Kadłubek records an early account of the dragon in his chronicle.

  2. Middle Ages → Modern era

    Chroniclers and popular tradition reshape the tale; the cobbler joins the story in the late 16th century.

  3. 1972

    Bronisław Chromy’s bronze dragon is installed beside the Dragon’s Den.

  4. Today

    The legend lives on at the foot of Wawel — and in the identity of BSides Kraków.

04 / Out of the story. Into the city.

Yes. You Can Visit the Dragon.

Polish sculptor Bronisław Chromy gave the dragon its bronze form. Installed in 1972, it stands beside the Vistula, below Wawel Castle, near the exit of the Dragon’s Den.

And yes, it breathes real fire.

06 / From Wawel to BSides

Why a Dragon?

Because this is Kraków.

The Wawel Dragon gives Security BSides Kraków an unmistakable connection to its city. The name says where we gather. The dragon carries a story that belongs here.

There is something familiar in that story for a room full of hackers: curiosity in the face of danger, taking on something bigger than yourself, and finding a way forward by thinking differently.

In the cobbler’s retelling, the dragon is defeated by understanding the problem, finding a weakness and exploiting it creatively.

Sounds familiar?

That is the connection we celebrate: local roots, shared knowledge and ingenuity. A story passed between generations meets a community that learns by sharing.

07 / A legend becomes an identity

Creating the
BSides Kraków Dragon

The final identity brings the dragon together with the BSides name and Kraków lettering. A red silhouette, a burst of orange fire, and bold, distressed type give the mark its character.

Read it as three ideas in one: the Wawel legend, the city and the security community. Its strong outline and compact composition suit the places a conference identity needs to live — badges, shirts, stickers, a website and physical objects.

This is a reading of the finished logo. No documented sketch-to-logo sequence is available here, so we are showing the existing identity rather than reconstructing a design process.

08 / Built in Kraków

A Dragon Belongs in Kraków.

BSides conferences exist all over the world.
This one could only exist here.

Security BSides Kraków carries a piece of the city in its identity: a creature that has lived beneath Wawel Hill in Polish stories for centuries.

Our dragon just happens to hack things now.
